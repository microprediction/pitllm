Reviews

Where a paper on the reading list does not support what it claims, and how much of it survives.

The ideas page summarises what each paper claims. This section records what did not hold up on checking. Eight papers have a finding substantial enough for its own page. Each names the exact claim and section, gives the counterexample or the missing assumption, and says what still stands.

A red note means a demonstrable error, a failed inference, or a material contradiction. Ordinary qualifications, for limited generalisability or an unverified priority claim, are folded into the summaries themselves rather than raised here. Three rules of thumb generate most of the findings below: a null experiment is not a universal impossibility proof, a diagnostic is not automatically an identified causal quantity, and a clean output is not evidence that hidden future information went unused.

PaperFindingWhat it comes to
Look-Ahead-Freedom as Temporal Non-Interference
The halting reduction does not gate the future value		 Proof error Section 7 claims the constructed pipeline leaks exactly when a simulated machine halts. Under the evaluation rules the paper gives in §6.2, the future value reaches the output in the never-halting case too, so the reduction does not establish what it is used for.
The Geometry of Forgetting
Orthogonality does not prove impossibility		 Invalid inference The paper infers that uncertainty-based detectors must be blind to staleness from near-orthogonal probe directions and the failure of the detectors it tested. Near-orthogonality does not support a universal impossibility claim.
Temporal Critique Fine-Tuning
Cutoff dependence is compatible with supervised learning		 Invalid inference The paper argues that supervised fine-tuning cannot teach temporal admissibility because admissibility depends on the cutoff. Supervised objectives condition on inputs, and the cutoff can be one of them. The paper's own appendix writes that objective.
TEMPO
The gradient alignment claim has a counterexample		 Proof error Proposition 1 asserts that the leakage-mode gradient descends the leakage cost. Its proof changes the weighting distribution without changing the centering means. Two independent witnesses make the inner product positive.
The Memorization Problem
Softmax reaches only the interior of the simplex		 Mathematical error, repairable Appendix C.3 claims finite real logits realize every distribution on a finite label set. That fails at the boundary. The error is local and repairable, and the paper's central non-identification result does not depend on it.
Temporal Leakage in LLM Backtesting
Three different clocks		 Definition mismatch Leakage is defined against the forecast as-of date, but Assumption 1 treats outcomes resolving after the training cutoff as clean. Those are different boundaries, and news in between can leak.
Simulated Ignorance Fails
A performance gap is not an amount of leakage		 Identification overclaim The headline 52% is the share of a performance gap left after a prompting intervention. The comparison that produces it changes the model as well as the cutoff, so the number is not an identified leakage quantity.
All Leaks Count, Some Count More
Influence under re-prompting is not internal provenance		 Attribution limitation Shapley-DCLR attributes influence to visible rationale claims by re-prompting on subsets of them. That measures the effect of a later prompt, not what caused the original answer.

Coverage and limits

All 72 paper entries on the ideas page were inventoried. Primary text was available for 68. The broad pass compared each summary against the abstract and conclusions where those could be extracted; selected papers received deeper checks of methods, assumptions and proofs. Four full texts were not reachable: StoriesLM, Sarkar & Vafa, Re(Visiting), and the historical-behavioural-science paper. Those are marked as access gaps and are not treated as verified.

This is a screening audit with targeted proof checks. It is not a line-by-line verification of every paper, and it reproduces no training experiments. “No error identified” is limited to the material inspected.

Reproducing the numerical checks

reviews/counterexamples.py uses only the Python standard library. It checks the TEMPO gradient, the orthogonality example, the mean-shift example and the missing gate in Fonseca's reduction, and asserts each result. reviews/verify_tempo_prop1.py computes the TEMPO inner product straight from the score functions and reports the dropped covariance term.
